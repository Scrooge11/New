# Outreach campaigns: Belmont life-estate holders and likely-inherited properties

Generated by `pipeline/build_campaign.py` from the lead lists. Each campaign folder holds:

| File | Use |
|---|---|
| `mailing_list.csv` / `.xlsx` | One row per household (owners at the same mailing address are combined so nobody gets five letters), with name, salutation, USPS address lines, every property they own, flags and priority. Upload to a print-and-mail service or mail-merge it yourself. |
| `skip_trace_export.csv` | One row per person, in the column layout skip-tracing services accept (BatchSkipTracing, PropStream, REISkip, TLO-based vendors). This is how you get phone numbers and emails; the assessor data has none. |
| `letters_<campaign>.docx` | Letter 1 already merged for every household, one per page, ready to print on letterhead or upload to Click2Mail / Lob / PostcardMania. Edit `templates/campaign_settings.json` (your name, phone, email, return address) and re-run to regenerate. |
| `../templates/` | Editable copy for letter 1, the follow-up letter, a postcard, an email (for after skip tracing), and a phone script. Merge fields are in double braces. |

The dashboard (Campaign tab) shows the same households with a status per household (not contacted, letter 1 sent, replied, interested, do not contact...), notes, a mailing-list export, and print-ready letters. Statuses are saved in the artifact's shared database, so phone and desktop stay in sync and Claude can report progress on request.

## Why start with life-estate holders

A life estate is created when an owner deeds the house to family (usually children) while keeping the right to live there for life. The people on this list are, almost by definition, longtime owners who have already done their succession planning. Two things follow:

* **The owner cannot sell alone.** The remaindermen (the family who will inherit) must sign the deed too. The letters therefore speak to the owner *and their family*, and every template invites them to involve family or an attorney. Expect the reply to come from a son or daughter.
* **Many Massachusetts life estates exist for MassHealth (Medicaid) planning.** Selling during the life tenant's lifetime can affect eligibility and how proceeds are split. Never advise on this; encourage them to check with their elder-law attorney. A respectful, no-pressure tone is not just polite here, it is what makes these deals close.

Households whose mailing address *is* the property are owner-occupied by an elderly owner; the absentee ones (owner elsewhere in Belmont, another town, or Florida) are often already living with family or in care, and are the warmest leads.

## Suggested sequence

1. **Letter 1** (the merged DOCX), first-class stamp, hand-addressed or window envelope. About $1 per piece self-mailed, $1.20-1.80 through a service. Mark households "Letter 1 sent" in the dashboard.
2. **Letter 2** 4-6 weeks later to everyone who has not replied or opted out.
3. **Postcard** about 3 months in, then repeat a light touch quarterly. Most responses come on the second or third touch.
4. **Skip trace** the non-responders you care most about and call by hand (see the phone script), after scrubbing the list against the National Do Not Call Registry and the Massachusetts Do Not Call list.
5. Log every reply in the dashboard the same day. "Do not contact" removes them from every future export.

## Rules to respect (Massachusetts and federal)

* **No deception, no fake urgency.** Massachusetts consumer-protection rules (M.G.L. c. 93A; 940 CMR 3) reach real-estate solicitations. Do not dress a letter up as an official notice, imply the owner must act, or mention tax problems or "estates" in a way that shames or alarms.
* **Say who you are.** If you hold a Massachusetts real-estate license, 254 CMR 3.00 requires solicitations to disclose that and name your brokerage. If you are an investor buying for yourself, say so plainly, as the templates do.
* **Calls and texts:** the TCPA and both Do Not Call lists apply. Hand-dialed calls only, 8 am to 8 pm, and honor "don't call" the first time.
* **Email:** CAN-SPAM (truthful subject, your postal address, working opt-out honored within 10 business days). The email template includes the required footer.
* **Elders:** many recipients are in their 80s. Massachusetts elder-protection law and unfair-practice rules treat undue pressure on elders harshly, and it is simply the wrong way to earn this business. Let them bring in family and an attorney, put everything in writing, and give them time.
* **Data:** assessor ownership records are public and may be used for marketing. Keep a permanent do-not-contact list and honor it across every channel.
