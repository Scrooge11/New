## 2. Demand drivers

### EV adoption (Massachusetts)
- **166,296 EVs on the road at end of 2025** (BEV + PHEV, light-duty) vs state targets of 200,000 by 2025 and 900,000 by 2030 — Mass.gov Clean Energy & Climate Metrics.
- Net additions: **35,651 in 2024** (2024 Climate Report Card); **>25,000 in 2025** (2025 Report Card via News From The States) — i.e., adoption "plateaued in 2025 after two years of growth" as federal incentives were rolled back. Record Nov–Dec 2024: ~11,000 registrations. Early-2025 EV sales −8% y/y (Tesla −29%) (Streetsblog MA).
- BEVs were 12.6% of new MA registrations in Q4 2023 (Alliance for Automotive Innovation via WBJ). Environment Massachusetts counts 91,100 EVs registered in 2024 (25× 2016 — likely BEV-only definition).
- Public charging: 10,303 ports end-2025 (+18%; DC fast +58%), 5th in the nation.
- **MOR-EV (state rebate, 2026):** $3,500 base (new ≤$55,000 MSRP or used ≤$40,000), +$1,500 MOR-EV+ income adder, +$1,000 ICE trade-in adder → up to $6,000. DOER proposed amendments (comment period Jul 31–Sep 4, 2026) to lower price caps and add an income threshold. No federal EV purchase credits remain in 2026.
- **Federal 30C home-charger credit: ENDED.** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act moved the sunset from 2032 to property placed in service by **June 30, 2026**; it was 30% up to $1,000 for homes in eligible (non-urban/low-income) tracts; installs completed by the deadline claim via Form 8911 on 2026 returns. Implication: H1-2026 pull-forward; no federal lever for H2-2026 sales — utility rebates are now the main close tool.
- **National Grid (MA) Residential EV Charging Upgrade Program:** wiring-upgrade rebate **up to $700** (single-family; more for 2–4 units), enhanced/"Turnkey" up to 100% of cost for Environmental Justice or R-2 discount-rate customers; requires enrollment in Off-Peak Charging (~$100/yr savings) and an **electrician's invoice** showing wiring/labor; 2026 form covers purchases/wiring Jan 1–Dec 10, 2026 (program page still shows an outdated Mar 31, 2026 deadline — confirm).
- **Eversource (MA):** up to **$700** toward wiring/panel work (standard rate); up to **$1,700** total for discount-rate customers including the charger; charger must be a Wi-Fi Level 2 ≤80A unit on Eversource's qualified list; since Mar 2, 2026 all rebate recipients must enroll in Managed Charging (DPU order Oct 2025, live Mar 2026). (Westford/Chelmsford/Littleton are largely National Grid territory with municipal utilities in Littleton and Groton — verify by address.)
- **Installation rules:** residential Level 2 chargers need an electrical permit under 527 CMR 12.00 (NEC Art. 625) from the local Inspector of Wires (typically $50–$150), dedicated 240V circuit with GFCI protection; work must be by a MA-licensed electrician (M.G.L. c.141 §1A). New construction under the Stretch Code (225 CMR 23.00) must be EV-ready/EV-capable.

### Heat pumps / electrification (panel-upgrade driver)
- Mass Save 2025: 30,567–31,700 heat-pump homes; 12,715 households fully replaced fossil heating; Mass Save reports $3.6B in 2025 benefits. State needs ~62,000 installs/yr by 2030 (WBUR) — roughly double the current pace.
- Current rebates (2026, third-party summaries): whole-home air-source **$2,650/ton up to $8,500** (partial-home $1,125/ton; ground-source $13,500); income-eligible enhanced tier up to **$16,000** ASHP / **$25,000** GSHP (masssave.com) with "necessary electrical panel updates" covered for qualifying households. Equipment must be installed by Dec 31, 2026 for the current cycle (paperwork due Feb 28, 2027); R-410A systems ineligible from Jan 1, 2026; rebates step down slightly in 2026–27 under the 2025–27 plan (WBUR). Third-party sites cite panel incentives of $2,000–$4,000 for electrification projects — **unverified; confirm with Mass Save/utility.** A standalone Mass Save panel rebate could not be confirmed.

### Standby generators / outages
- Recent MA storm peaks: ~285,000–300,000 customers out (NBC Boston / CBS Boston storms, dates not confirmed); 276,000+ in a blizzard (WHDH, ©2025); 222,000+ in the Oct 2021 nor'easter; a 2026 nor'easter peaked ~50,000 (19,000 National Grid customers still out next day). EIA SAIDI (all events): Massachusetts 163.2 min (2022) vs 518.7 (2021); New England 419.6 min (2024) vs 480.5 (2023).
- Demand is outage-driven and lumpy: Generac Q4-2025 home-standby shipments −25% after a quiet 2025 storm year; ServiceTitan data show installs +25.8% in the 2024 hurricane year then back to 2022–23 levels in 2025. Boston-area contractors sell $400–$500/yr maintenance plans (recurring revenue).

### Aging housing stock
- Westford: median year built **1983**; of 9,241 units, 1,464 (15.8%) pre-1940, 79 (1940s), 772 (1950s), 967 (1960s), 891 (1970s) → **~4,170 units (~45%) built before 1980** (Point2Homes table; TopHap median 1984).
- Chelmsford: median year built **1965** (TopHap). Lowell: **42.7% built before 1939**, 21.4% 1940–69, 25.6% 1970–99, 10.3% 2000+ (NeighborhoodScout) → ~64% pre-1970. Middlesex County: median year built 1959 (TopHap listings); 666,655 housing units (Census QuickFacts V2023).
- Pre-1980 homes typically carry 60–100A services, fuse/FPE/Zinsco panels, ungrounded or aluminum branch circuits — the core base for service changes, rewires and code-driven remodel electrical.

### Home renovation spending
- JCHS LIRA (mid-2026 release): owner remodeling/repair spending ~**$519B** annualized through mid-2027 with y/y growth decelerating to **~0.5% by Q2 2027**; "remodeling permitting and retail building-product spending have flattened." April 2026 release: 2.9% growth early 2026 easing to 1.6% by year-end. Late-2025 release: $513B (2025Q1) → $526B (2026Q1).
- JCHS *Improving America's Housing 2025*: market exceeded $600B post-pandemic and remains ~50% above pre-pandemic, supported by aging homes/households and record home equity. Boston has long ranked among the top-5 metros for per-owner improvement spending (~$4,900/owner, 2015 data).

## 5. Labor

- **BLS OEWS, Massachusetts electricians (47-2111):** median **$82,120** (May 2024) vs US $62,350; May 2025: median $79,420, mean $81,560, 10th–90th pct **$46,990–$128,210**, employment **17,810** (secondary sites quoting BLS). Boston metro mean ~$83,370 (OEWS 2025 via Hyring).
- **Lowell-Billerica-Chelmsford, MA-NH (NECTA division):** latest retrievable BLS table is May 2017 — 1,670 electricians, mean $28.55/hr / $59,380/yr (May 2016: 1,540; $58,660). Current figures sit on bls.gov (blocked here); expect ~35–40% higher wages today based on statewide growth.
- **Journeyman pay (PayScale 2026):** Boston avg **$38.59/hr** (10th–90th: $33.99–$60.64); experienced $39.71/hr ($37.15–$44.22 IQR); Worcester $29.68/hr. Boston master electrician ~$37.48/hr base (employee, not billing rate). Fully loaded employer cost will run ~1.3–1.4× wage (own estimate — insurance, workers' comp, payroll tax).
- **Shortage data:** BLS ~81,000 openings/yr (2024–34) / ~72,700 (2025–35), mostly replacement; ~10,000 exit vs ~7,000 enter annually; ~30% of union electricians near retirement; MassCEC (2023) projects MA needs ~29,700 additional FTE clean-energy workers by 2030 (38,100 trained), with 88% of employers reporting hiring difficulty (2022); AIM: MA jobs +21% by 2030 vs workforce +1.5%. Counterpoint: IBEW Local 103 says no shortage exists for union contractors. Implication for a lean shop: hiring a journeyman is costly and slow; subcontracting and apprentices (1 apprentice per journeyman ratio applies) are the realistic growth path.

## 6. Regulation (Massachusetts)

**Electrician licensing (237 CMR; Board of State Examiners of Electricians, DOL).** Journeyman exam: ≥8,000 documented hours over ≥4 years under a licensed journeyman + 600 hours classroom (237 CMR 13.03, 22.01). Master exam: hold a MA journeyman license ≥1 year of active work + 150 hours classroom (237 CMR 13.01, 22.02). Renewal every 3 years with 21 CE hours, 15 of them on MA code amendments/business/law (237 CMR 17.01). Reciprocity without exam for some states (237 CMR 15; master path needs 5 years' experience + current MA journeyman license). A master license is the credential under which the company contracts and pulls electrical permits.

**Electrical code.** 527 CMR 12.00 (Massachusetts Electrical Code) adopts the **2023 NEC** (NFPA 70 as issued Aug 12, 2022) with MA amendments, effective for permits granted after **Feb 28, 2023** (Rule 7); the applicable edition vests with the electrical permit application date. A 2026-NEC adoption was not confirmed in this research.

**Electrical permits.** M.G.L. c.141 §1A requires anyone in the business of installing wiring to be licensed; c.143 §3L requires notice/permit for work and completion notice to the Inspector of Wires; towns require a valid MA license and workers' comp affidavit with each permit (e.g., Montgomery, Boston). Fees vary by town (Boston: $20 + $1/outlet, service upgrades $20 + $0.25/amp; West Boylston $195 for a new single-family; re-inspections ~$75). Homeowner self-permits are inconsistent town to town; a licensed contractor avoids the issue.

**Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration (OCABR, M.G.L. c.142A).** Required for anyone soliciting, bidding or performing residential contracting on an existing **owner-occupied 1–4 unit** home where the project exceeds **$1,000** — this covers JS's kitchen/bath/basement remodels. Register online via MA Contractor Hub: **$150** registration + Guaranty Fund fee **$100** (0–3 employees), $200 (4–10), $300 (11–30), $500 (>30); renew every 2 years ($100). The Guaranty Fund pays homeowners up to $10,000 (state guide; Boston's page says $25,000) for unpaid judgments; homeowners may use the state arbitration program only against registered contractors. HIC ≠ CSL.

**Construction Supervisor License (CSL, OPSI/780 CMR).** Needed to pull building permits and supervise structural work (e.g., cutting/notching framing, removing walls) in 1–2 family homes (Restricted CSL) or all buildings (Unrestricted); specialty CSLs exist. Secondary sources: 3 years' experience + PSI exam, 2-year renewal; MA does not recognize out-of-state licenses. For remodels involving structure, JS must hold a CSL or partner with a CSL holder/homeowner who pulls the building permit; electrical/plumbing permits remain separate.

**Home improvement contract rules (c.142A).** Contracts over $1,000 must be written and include: HIC registration number on page one, detailed scope, total price and payment schedule, start and substantial-completion dates, lien/security-interest disclosure, a 10-point-bold "do not sign if blanks" warning, and the homeowner's 3-business-day cancellation right (c.93 §48 / c.140D §10 / c.255D §14; applies when signed away from the contractor's place of business). **Deposit before work may not exceed one-third of the contract price** (or the cost of special-order materials); no final payment may be demanded until completion. Violations are automatic c.93A violations (treble damages + attorney fees).

## 7. Seasonality (New England)

- **Winter trough (Jan–Feb):** residential electrical slows after the holidays in cold climates; large projects pause (TCNECA; Pixlel). Interior remodel work (kitchens, basements) can be deliberately shifted into winter — a Waltham builder recommends scheduling electricians/painters in February rather than August.
- **Generators:** installs are constrained when the ground is frozen (slab/trenching), so installs cluster Apr–Nov; orders spike after major outages and around Labor Day as homeowners anticipate winter (Maine contractor: post-storm waits stretched to September). Storm years swing volume ±25%.
- **Spring–fall peak (Apr–Oct):** exterior/service work, panel changes, additions, and remodel starts; summer A/C and thunderstorm-driven service calls.
- **Q4 deadline pushes:** Mass Save heat-pump equipment must be installed by Dec 31, 2026 (paperwork Feb 28, 2027); National Grid EV rebate window closes Dec 10, 2026; MOR-EV and utility rebate cycles reset Jan 1. The federal 30C sunset (Jun 30, 2026) pulled EV-charger demand into H1 2026 — expect softer H2 2026 charger volume unless utility rebates are marketed actively (inference).
- **EV chargers:** tied to vehicle deliveries, which in MA peaked in Nov–Dec 2024 (~11,000 registrations); demand is otherwise year-round and indoor — a good winter filler.
- Market backdrop: JCHS projects remodeling growth slowing to ~0.5% y/y by mid-2027, so winter cash-flow planning and recurring maintenance (generator plans, Mass Save/EV follow-ons) matter.
