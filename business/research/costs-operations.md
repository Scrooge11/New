## 2. Insurance (small MA electrical contractor)

- **General liability $1M/$2M:** $500–$1,200/yr typical, Insureon median $684/yr ($57/mo); solo range to $2,500; completed-operations is essential for electrical. [24]
- **Commercial auto:** electricians avg $1,682/yr (Insureon); $1,500–$3,050 per vehicle; contractors overall $3,173/yr (May 2026); Progressive contractor avg $260/mo. GCs typically require $1M auto liability. [25]
- **Workers' comp (MA class 5190 Electrical Wiring):** WCRIBMA lookup shows rate **$1.475 per $100 payroll, minimum premium $260, effective 7/1/2024** (prior: $2.175/$285 in 2022, $1.845/$273 in 2023; $2.05/$281 in 2020). The MA SJC remanded the 14.6% 2024 rate cut, so rates may be revised; carriers also apply deviations/credits. **(derived)** $75k journeyman payroll → ~$1,100/yr at $1.475 (~$1,540 at $2.05); $120k payroll → $1,770–$2,460. A third-party estimate of $438–$625/mo on $150k payroll ($5.3–7.5k/yr) is far above MA's filed rate. EMR applies only ≥$5k premium; pay-as-you-go available. Exact decimal (1.475 vs 1.47 + loss constant) **(unverified)**. [26][32][33]
- **Tools / inland marine:** Insureon median $169/yr, electrician avg $494/yr; ~$0.80 per $100 of equipment ($800 per $100k). Shop policies stop covering tools once the van leaves. [27]
- **Umbrella $1M:** $400–$1,500/yr lower-risk trades; general commercial $1,000–$5,000; contractor-focused broker $3,000–$6,000 per $1M. [34]
- **Bonds:** MA has no state license bond for electricians; some cities require contractor/street-opening bonds (e.g., Boston DPW); vendor MA license bonds up to $25k from ~$100/yr. [35]
- **Builder's risk (remodels):** 1–4% of project value (renovations 2–4%); small kitchen/bath often at carrier minimum ~$400–$500 or $100–$300/mo. [36]
- **Program total:** contractor guides cite $5,000–$12,000/yr (solo-to-small incl. auto, tools) or $2,000–$6,000 for a small electrical shop. **(derived) JS yr-1:** GL $900 + auto $2,000 + WC $1,200 + inland marine $400 + umbrella $1,000 ≈ **$5,500/yr**, plus builder's risk per remodel. [24][37]

## 4. Operating benchmarks

- **Gross margin:** service-focused electrical 30–50% (SimPro); residential service 35–42% for $500k–$2M shops (Lightning Path); 55%+ on residential service calls vs 25% on competitive commercial bids (bookkeeping firm); specialty subs 26–34%. ServiceTitan's price-book target is 65–67% GP. [45][46][47][48]
- **Net margin:** NECA median 5–6%, top quartile 10–12% (via SimPro); vendor "healthy" range 10–20%; residential service 8–14%; many shops at 2–3%. [45][46][48]
- **Revenue per field tech:** $250k–$400k service benchmark (SimPro); $207k North American commercial-service average, best-in-class +65% (BuildOps). [45][49]
- **Billable hours per tech:** 2,080 paid → ~1,704 billable after PTO/holidays/training/drive; 1,600 conservative (PrimeX); ServiceTitan example 583 hrs at 30% utilization, 50% = "extremely efficient". Plan 1,000–1,400 billable for an owner who also sells/estimates **(derived)**. [50][51]
- **Average ticket:** residential service call $200–$400 (small repairs ~65% of residential job count); basic service $350–$650 (Pulse, unsourced); one MA-adjacent shop: $1,100 average electrical ticket, $400 generator service; EV L2 $1,500–$4,000; standby generator $8k–$18k. [52][53][54]
- **Material markup:** industry standard 2x–6x cost (ServiceTitan); practitioners 50–150%, ~100% average; lower on big-ticket items; ~2x = 50% gross margin on parts. [47]
- **Overhead rate:** 13–20% of sales; smallest firms ~21.6% (ECM, dated 2002). [55]
- **DSO / collections:** commercial subs 56-day DSO (Billd 2025); 96 days invoice-to-pay (Siteline 2025); residential service should collect on completion by card/ACH (0–15 days) **(derived)**. [56]
- **Deposits / progress payments (MA):** M.G.L. c.142A requires a written contract for home-improvement work >$1,000 on 1–4 family homes with 14 items; **deposit may not exceed one-third of the contract price or the actual cost of special-order/custom materials**; no final payment demand before completion; HIC number on page 1; violations are c.93A (treble damages + fees). Common schedules: 1/3–1/3–1/3; 10/30/30/30; 15/20/25/25/15 by milestone; 5–10% retainage; special-order materials billed when ordered. [57][58][59]
- **Warranty:** 1–2 years labor typical, 1 year most common; new-home programs put wiring in the 2-year "systems" tier; manufacturer warranties pass through; void if others alter the work. [60]

## 5. Revenue benchmarks

- **Solo ceiling:** one-man shop "$250–300k absolute max total sales," owner paying himself ~$90k (Mike Holt forum); owner salaries $60k–$150k nationally, solos lower early (Housecall Pro). No published first-year benchmark. [61][62]
- **(derived) First year:** 1,000–1,200 billable hrs × $125–$150 = $125k–$180k labor + materials at 2x = **$170k–$250k** with an existing customer base; $120k–$180k from a cold start.
- **(derived) Year 3 (2 techs + owner selling):** 2 × $175k–$250k/tech = **$350k–$500k**; $600k+ if remodel GC revenue (subs pass-through) is included.
- **2–3 person shops:** $207k–$400k per tech implies $400k–$750k; StatCan 2024 average electrical contractor (revenue $30k–$5M) $613.5k, cost of sales ~55% of revenue. [45][49][63]
- **Service vs install share:** no hard split found. Small repairs are ~65% of residential jobs by count [52], but installs (EV $1.2–4k, panels $2.5–8k, generators $12–20k) dominate dollars; a 30/70 service/install revenue mix is a reasonable planning assumption **(derived)**.
- **Seasonality (Northeast):** busiest May–Aug with a secondary Nov–Dec bump; residential remodel slowest Jan–Feb; "electrician near me" searches peak July (+26%); Aug→Sep calls/revenue dip ~7% (ServiceTitan, all trades); winter cold snaps raise service calls; storms override (electrical revenue doubled post-Ida); one Ohio EC "extremely slow until May" after a hard winter. Generator and panel work are the winter fillers. [64][65][66]

Fixed caps 14.75%/13.75%/12.75%/11.75%. 10-yr term without real estate; 5/10-yr Treasury and SOFR added as base rates 3/1/2026; FY2026 fee schedule expired 10/1/2026 — confirm FY2027 fees. **(derived)** $100k @ 12.75%/10 yr = $1,478/mo; $150k = $2,218/mo. [67][68]
- **SBA microloan:** ≤$50k (avg ~$13–15k), ~8–13%, up to 6–7 yrs via nonprofit intermediaries. **(derived)** $50k @ 10%/6 yr = $926/mo. [69]
- **MGCC:** microloans $5k–$100k; term loans to $2M at ~10% fixed; LOC prime+1.75% (listing undated — verify; some programs exclude startups). [70]
- **NCMDC** (Middlesex/Worcester/Franklin): SBA microlender; loans up to $50k historically, recent releases cite up to $150k–$250k; working capital, equipment, leaseholds; rates unpublished (978-353-7607). [71]
- **Mill Cities Community Investments** (Lowell Community Loan Fund CDFI; Merrimack Valley, Essex, Middlesex): small-business startup/expansion loans; rates unpublished. [72]
- **Van/equipment financing:** Ford Credit commercial promos 7.3%/60, 7.4%/72, 7.5%/84 mo (expired 9/30/26) and 5.9%/72 standard; GM Financial 6.59%/72; market 5–15% APR (700+ FICO) to 6–22%; <680 FICO adds 2–5 pts; equipment financing 4–45% APR. [73][74]
- **Lines of credit:** bank median 6.90% fixed / 7.27% variable (KC Fed, Q1 2026); bank average 7.59–7.86%; online lenders 6–57%. [75]
- **What lenders expect:** DSCR ≥1.25x (some accept 1.15x; 1:1 with personal obligations); equity injection 10% minimum for startups, often 15–20%; FICO mid-600s+; collateral (van/equipment + personal); industry experience; documented use of funds and projections; injection source verified, seller/standby debt only. [76][77]

## 7. Pricing models

- **Flat rate vs T&M:** flat rate gives up-front price certainty, easier comparison, controlled margins, rewards fast techs, fewer disputes; risks underpricing unknowns and needs a maintained price book. T&M suits troubleshooting/open scope and remodel extras but customers watch the clock and totals creep. Rule: flat-rate repeatable tasks (outlets, fixtures, circuits, EV, panels); T&M for diagnostics after a flat diagnostic fee. [78][79]
- **Diagnostic / service fee:** Lowell MA $75–$175 trip/service fee for daytime; national $79–$179; most shops $100–$200; emergency up to $300 + premium rate. [80][81][82]
- **Hourly rates:** Boston $55–$110/hr (Angi), $50–$100 (HomeAdvisor); residential 2026 $75–$150/hr; first hour billed higher for travel. Labor cost: Boston journeyman median $38.59/hr (Payscale); MA median electrician $79,420/yr (BLS via secondary). **(derived)** $40/hr wage ≈ $55–60/hr loaded; at 60% utilization breakeven ≈ $100/hr before overhead → target **$150–$185/hr effective**. [82][83][84]
- **EV charger pricing:** tiers simple $800–$1,500; mid $1,500–$3,000; complex with panel/sub-panel/trench $3,000–$8,000+; paid site assessment $150–$300; price = (labor + materials) × 2.0–2.5 to net 10–20% (vendor rule); run length is the biggest cost driver; MA market $1,200–$3,000 installed (Newton installer: $500–$1,500 labor + $500–$800 charger); Eversource rebate $700–$1,400, Mass Save up to $700; federal 30C reportedly expired 6/30/2026 **(unverified)**. [85][86][87]
- **Panel upgrades (MA):** $2,500–$8,000 typical, ~$4,000 average; panel-only $1,800–$3,500 vs full service upgrade $4,000–$8,000; wiring permits $100–$500 by town; 2–4 weeks with utility coordination; bundle EV circuit while in the panel. [87][88]
- **Standby generators:** Boston whole-home $12k–$22k; national 22 kW $7k–$23k (avg $12–14k); transfer switch $1,300–$1,733; maintenance plans $400–$500/yr. [89]
- **Flat-rate reference points:** outlet $100–$150; fixture $150–$250; new circuit $300–$600; room rewire $1,200–$3,000. [79]
