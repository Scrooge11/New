The 12-Point Weekly Pool Maintenance Checklist Every Florida Pool Needs

July 14, 2026 · 7 min read · By the Blue Water Pool team

Ask anyone who has moved here from up north: South Florida is the hardest place in America to keep a pool clear. Year-round sun destroys chlorine, summer storms dump debris and dilute chemistry, and warm water grows algae like it's being paid to. The pools that stay swim-ready all have one thing in common — a real weekly routine, not "when it looks like it needs it."

Below is the exact 12-point checklist our technicians run on every visit. Use it yourself, or use it to audit whoever you're paying.

The cleaning work (points 1–5)

1. Skim the surface

Floating debris is easy to dismiss because the skimmer "will get it." Some of it, yes — the rest waterlogs and sinks, where it stains surfaces and feeds algae. Two minutes with a net prevents an hour of vacuuming later.

2. Brush walls, steps, and the waterline

Algae doesn't start as a green pool; it starts as an invisible biofilm on walls and steps. Weekly brushing disrupts it before it blooms. The waterline tile also collects sunscreen and body oils that turn into a stubborn scum ring if ignored.

3. Vacuum the floor

Whether it's a manual vac, a robot, or a pressure cleaner, the floor needs attention weekly — especially in the dead spots circulation misses, usually corners and steps. That's where sunk debris quietly composts.

4. Empty skimmer and pump baskets

A packed skimmer basket chokes flow, which weakens filtration, which clouds water. A packed pump basket makes your pump work harder and die younger. Both take thirty seconds to empty.

5. Leave the area cleaner than you found it

Debris pulled from a pool shouldn't end up scattered on the deck. Small thing; tells you everything about the standard of the person doing the work.

The chemistry work (points 6–8)

6. Test before touching anything

Chlorine, pH, and alkalinity, minimum — measured, not eyeballed. Dumping chlorine into a pool you haven't tested is the drive-by move that makes water harsh one week and unsafe the next.

7. Balance to targets, not vibes

Free chlorine 1–3 ppm, pH 7.4–7.6, alkalinity 80–120 ppm. Florida-specific reality: intense UV means stabilizer (cyanuric acid) matters here more than almost anywhere — but too much of it locks up your chlorine. Balance is a weekly discipline, not a monthly correction.

8. Check salt systems properly

"It's a salt pool, it takes care of itself" has funded a lot of resurfacing jobs. Salt cells drift, scale up, and quietly stop producing. Weekly verification of output — and salinity in range — is the difference between a salt system that pampers you and one that ambushes you.

The equipment work (points 9–11)

9. Read the filter pressure

The gauge on your filter is a health monitor. Pressure creeping 8–10 psi above its clean baseline means the filter is loading up and flow is dropping. Catch it weekly and a cleaning restores it; ignore it and you get cloudy water and a strained pump.

10. Look and listen at the equipment pad

Drips at fittings, a pump growling instead of humming, a timer knocked off schedule by a power blink — all one-minute observations, all cheap to fix early and expensive to fix late.

11. Scan for what's changed

A good technician notices the new stain, the light ring starting to rust, the spot the cleaner keeps missing. Weekly eyes on the pool is how $50 problems stay $50 problems.

The accountability work (point 12)

12. Document the visit

We finish every service with a photo report to the homeowner. If you maintain your own pool, keep a simple log of readings and doses — patterns in the numbers predict problems weeks early. If you pay for service, expect proof. "Trust me, I was there" is not a maintenance record.

DIY or hire it out?

This checklist takes a diligent homeowner 45–60 minutes a week, every week, including the weeks it's 95°F, the weeks it storms daily, and the weeks you're on vacation — and the consistency matters more than the effort. If that sounds fine, this list is everything you need. If it sounds like the chore you'll abandon by August, that's exactly what a real weekly service is for.
