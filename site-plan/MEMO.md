# 199 Central Street, Concord MA - fitting the AFAB house on the lot

Prepared 22 Sept 2026 from two documents:

* **Existing Conditions Plan** (PLACES Associates, Inc., project 5827, plan 5827-ECP, dated 11 Sept 2026, 1" = 10'). Lot: Assessor's Parcel 2559, 7,511 sf, 73.4' frontage on Central Street, Residence C district. The plan already shows the zoning setback lines: 20' front, 15' sides, 23.2' rear.
* **AFAB Enterprises "New Single Family Residence"** plan set (file `Carter.pdf`, sheets 1, 3, 4, 5, 1/4" = 1'-0"): a 40'-0" x 45'-0" two-story main body, with a 24'-wide garage bay and a 16'-wide covered porch both projecting 7'-6" toward the street (52'-6" overall), a 16' x 14' rear deck and a 5'-4" x 6'-2" bulkhead. 3,387 sf of floor area (1,400 + 1,987).

The deliverable is `output/199-Central-St_Proposed-Site-Plan.pdf`: four 18x24 sheets drawn on top of the surveyor's sheet at the same scale, so they can be printed and compared directly with the ECP. Sheet PNG previews are alongside it.

> Concept overlay only. It is not a survey and not a permit drawing. Distances were scaled from the ECP vector linework (about +/- 0.3'), and the zoning text below comes from bylaw excerpts that should be confirmed with the Building Commissioner before anything is submitted.

## Bottom line

1. **Yes, set the house parallel with the side lot lines.** The side lines lean about 6 degrees off square to Central Street, so the strip between the two 15' side setback lines is only ~42.4' wide measured square to the sides. The 40' house clears both side setbacks only when it is rotated to match the sides (6.3 degrees clockwise on the sheet), and then with just 1.0' of wall clearance at the rear corners and 1.3' at the front. Square to the street it is ~1.1' over the side setback line at two opposite corners (Sheet 3).

   **Roof overhang:** with the owner's rule of a 12" side eave plus a 3" margin (1'-3" beyond each side wall), a 40'-wide body does not fit. The eave line lands 3" inside the 15' side setback at both rear corners and clears it by less than an inch at the front corners. The most a 40' body can hold is 12.4" per side. To keep 1'-3" on both sides the main body must be **39'-6" wide** (39.56' is the limit), or the side overhang must drop to about 9" over the rear 37' of the house. Rotation does not help; parallel is already the best angle.

2. **The plan is too deep for this lot in any orientation.** Because the front and rear setback lines are not square to the rotated house, a 40'-wide rectangle can be at most ~46.3' deep inside the envelope. The 45' main body fits with 1.3' of slide. The 7'-6" garage/porch projection cannot be held inside: with the main body pushed back onto the rear setback line the garage bay still sits 4.5' inside the 20' front yard (plan mirrored) or 6.2' (plan as drawn).

3. **Mirror the plan so the garage is on the west** (Sheet 1). It cuts the garage encroachment from 6.2' to 4.5', puts the driveway on the side that already has the curb cut, the existing drive and the electric service poles, and keeps the drive and excavation away from the 50" tree on the east line. The covered porch then faces the east neighbour.

4. **The rear deck and the bulkhead cannot be built as drawn.** The main body's rear wall has to sit on the rear setback line, so anything attached behind it is in the rear yard, and Concord measures rear yards to any structure except uncovered steps and ramps (Sec. 6.2.8). The 14' deck would be 13' inside the rear setback. Grade falls about 5' from the street to the rear line, so the practical answer is a walk-out lower level with a patio at grade instead of a first-floor deck, and a walk-out door instead of a bulkhead.

5. **A footprint that fits** (Sheet 4): keep the mirrored placement, cut the garage bay projection to 3'-0" and the porch roof to a 1'-3" entry canopy. The main body, garage and porch then all comply. The cost is a garage bay of about 18'-10" clear instead of 23'-4", so AFAB would need to push the garage rear wall ~1'-2" into the mudroom zone (or shorten the main body by 4'-6" and keep the 7'-6" front). Uncovered front steps and a stoop can still project.

6. **Check the size limits before redesigning around the setbacks.** The lot is pre-existing nonconforming (7,511 sf < 10,000 sf; 73.4' < 80'). A replacement house over 150% of the existing floor area needs a ZBA special permit (Sec. 7.1.5 / 11.6), which the ZBA routinely grants when setbacks are met. Separately, Concord caps residential floor area per acre (Table III / Sec. 6.2.13) and the Residence C figure was not obtainable; if it is anything like the Residence B example in the ZBA minutes (~0.29 x lot area) the allowance here would be ~2,160 sf against 3,387 sf proposed. That would decide the house size before the setbacks do.

## Why "parallel with the lot" is right

* West line S 4-43-20 E and east line S 5-29-50 E lean 6.7 and 5.9 degrees to the east as they run away from Central Street (S 78-35-22 W). The two 15' side setback lines are 42.9' apart along the front setback line and 42.1' apart along the rear one.
* A 40'-wide house square to the street needs 40' + 45' x tan(6.3 deg) = 45.0' of that strip over its 45' depth. It does not fit. Rotated to match the sides it needs 40.0' and fits with ~1.0' spare per side.
* Side clearance of ~1.0' at the rear corners is less than the 1'-3" eave-plus-margin the owner wants. See the roof-overhang section below.
* The house ends up skewed ~6 degrees to the street. The existing house is square to the street, but it is only 27.5' wide; at 40' the skew is the price of the width, and it is barely perceptible from the road.

## Roof overhang

Rule applied: 12" side eave + 3" margin = 1'-3" beyond each side wall, measured to the 15' side setback lines. The eave line is drawn in purple on Sheets 1, 2 and 4 with its own dimensions.

| | West lot line | East lot line |
|---|---|---|
| Eave line at front corners | 15.05' | 15.09' |
| Eave line at rear corners | 14.76' | 14.79' |

* The envelope narrows toward the rear (the two side lines are not quite parallel), so the walls have 1.3' spare at the front corners but only 1.0' at the rear. With 1'-3" of eave allowance the rear ~37' of eave on each side sits up to 3" inside the setback line.
* A 40'-0" body can hold at most 12.4" per side here. With 1'-3" per side the body can be at most 39.56' wide. Rotating away from parallel makes it worse at every angle tested.
* Two ways to comply: narrow the main body to **39'-6"** (6" out of the plan, e.g. the 16' living/dining bay becomes 15'-6"), or keep 40' and hold the side overhang to about **9"** on the rear 37' of the house (a 9" eave plus the 3" margin fits the 1.0' rear clearance exactly, so that is a zero-tolerance answer; 39'-6" is the safer one).
* Front and rear rake overhangs are not allowed for in these numbers. If the Town also measures the gable-end overhangs, the rear wall would have to come forward another 1'-3" and the depth problem below gets worse by the same amount. Ask the Building Commissioner how eaves are treated and get the text of the 3' side-yard exception in Sec. 6.2.7 (it covers low, <= 15'-high parts of the principal structure in Residence C and may or may not cover eaves).

## Ways to resolve the depth problem

Pick one; they are alternatives, not a sequence.

* **A. Redesign the front (Sheet 4).** Garage bay projection 3'-0", porch roof 1'-3" plus uncovered stoop and steps. Garage rear wall moves ~1'-2" into the mudroom zone to keep a 20' bay; otherwise the bay is 18'-10". Everything enclosed complies; only the deck/bulkhead question remains.
* **B. Shorten the main body** from 45' to 40'-6" and keep the 7'-6" front as drawn. Same result on the site plan, different floor-plan surgery (the 26' kitchen/family zone is the obvious place to take it).
* **C. Ask the ZBA.** The project needs a Sec. 7.1.5 special permit anyway if it exceeds 150% of the existing floor area, so the front-yard relief could be bundled into the same hearing. Two things weaken that case: Concord's bylaw allows nothing but uncovered steps, ramps, walls and fences in a front yard, and the new encroachments would be new. One thing helps: the existing porch roof is already 16.6' from the street (the ECP shows it), so the lot has a ~3.4' front-yard nonconformity today; holding the new front elements at or behind that 16.6' line is a much easier ask than 13.8'. Option 1's garage bay (15.5') is close to it; its porch (13.8') is not.
* **D. Size first.** If the Table III floor-area cap forces a smaller house, take the reduction out of the depth and the problem disappears.

## Rear deck and bulkhead

* The rear yard is measured to the nearest point of any structure attached to the dwelling; only uncovered steps and ramps are exempt (Sec. 6.2.8). With the rear wall on the line, a 14' deck is 13' inside the setback and the bulkhead ~4'.
* Grade: street el. 155, house area 154-155, rear line 150-151. A first-floor deck at the rear would stand roughly 5-6' above grade at its rail; a 6'-2" bulkhead would be tall. AFAB's own note ("bulkhead size may vary depending on existing topography") anticipates this.
* Recommendation: walk-out lower level at the rear with a patio at grade (no structure in the rear yard), a walk-out door instead of the bulkhead, and uncovered steps from the kitchen door if a landing is wanted. If a first-floor deck is essential, it needs ZBA relief.

## Driveway, trees, utilities

* **Driveway.** Option 1 puts the garage doors roughly 20-40' east of the west lot line, so the new apron is a straight shot from the doors to the street; the existing west-side curb cut can be widened into it, kept as a parking pad, or closed and restored. Option 2 needs a new curb cut on the east side over the water service and the wood ramp. Either needs a CPW driveway permit.
* **Trees.** Concord's Tree Preservation Bylaw protects trees >= 6" DBH whose trunks are in the setback strips (front 20', sides 15', rear 23.2'): here 50"D, 24"E, 24"E, 18"E, 18"E, 18"E, 18"E, 12"E, 10"D, 8"D. Option 1 takes out two 18"E in the front yard plus a 10"D at the side (46" protected DBH; mitigation is half that, 23", by replanting 2" caliper trees or $375 per inch not replanted, i.e. up to ~$8,600). Option 2 takes the 24"E instead of one 18"E. The 50" tree on the east line is ~15' from the nearest new wall; it needs an arborist's root-zone plan but is not in the footprint.
* **Utilities.** The water service enters at the middle of the frontage, the electric drop comes from the poles at the SW corner across the front yard, and the sewer/gas are in the street. The concrete-curb retaining wall and the bituminous pad at the rear are inside the new footprint or the rear yard and go.
* **Grading.** The rear of the new house sits 3-4' above the rear yard; plan the foundation as a walk-out or step the footings (AFAB's typical footing-step detail).

## How this was produced

* `geometry.py` reads the lot corners from the ECP's vector linework (the six boundary calls close to within 0.05' of the deed/measured distances; the computed lot area is 7,512 sf against the surveyor's 7,511 sf) and builds the setback envelope exactly where the surveyor drew it. The rear setback line as drawn is 0.3' tighter than its 23.2' label, so reported rear distances are referenced to the label.
* `analysis.py` computes, for a given rotation and mirror, the set of positions in which the 40' x 45' main body sits inside the envelope, and reports distances for every element; it also finds the largest garage/porch projection that fits, the widest body that fits with a 1'-3" eave allowance per side, and the largest eave allowance a 40' body can hold.
* `make_site_plan.py` draws the placements on a copy of the ECP page and writes the PDF and PNG previews. Re-run with `python3 make_site_plan.py` (needs `pymupdf` and `shapely`; set `ECP_PDF` to the path of the ECP file).
